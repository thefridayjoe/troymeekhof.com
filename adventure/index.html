Adventure

Here’s where the truck has been, what I sleep in, and how the footage gets made.

One trip: Michigan to Montana

One example of many: 4,500 miles in 9 days to Glacier National Park, no FSD.

I left Caledonia, Michigan, drove west, camped out of the truck, and came home with a film and a range spreadsheet. The trip became the most-read thing I’ve written and a full overlanding film on YouTube. It’s the reason people started calling me The Cybertruck Guy in a tone that wasn’t sarcastic.

Everything I learned about charging, camping, and keeping a camera battery alive in a truck bed is in the article: Driving Cybertruck from Michigan to Montana: 4,500 Miles in 9 Days.

Where else the truck has been

  • Silver Lake Sand Dunes, Michigan. I led the Tesla Owners Club of Michigan’s Silver Lake Cybertruck Dune Day on Oct 6, 2024. A line of stainless trucks on the only ORV dunes east of the Mississippi. Event thread.
  • Big Sur and the Pacific Coast Highway. Bixby Bridge, the cliffs, and a stainless truck that looks strangely at home next to the ocean.
  • Joshua Tree, California. Boulders, desert light, and two Cybertrucks parked where the rocks start.
  • Canadian Rockies. Lake Louise and the roads around it. Charging in Alberta is fine. The scenery is not fine. It’s unreasonable.
  • Ontario Crown land. Free camping, no reservations, no cell signal. See the Starlink note below.
  • Glacier National Park, Montana. The turnaround point on the Montana trip.
  • Michigan, constantly. Lake Michigan beaches, boat launches, two-tracks, and any cheap breakfast joint within range.

Camping with the truck

I sleep in the bed. The tonneau makes it a hard-shell tent. The 48V system and the outlets in the bed run the essentials, and I installed a Starlink Mini on the truck using that 48V power so I can upload from a Crown land site with no bars. The install guide is here: Install a Starlink Mini on a Cybertruck Using 48V Power.

Winter is its own sport. I’ve driven the truck below 0°F and documented what that does to range, charging, and the door handles: Guide to Driving Cybertruck in Extreme Cold. If you want the numbers instead of the narrative: Winter Tire Range Test at 70 MPH.

How the footage gets made

I’m a filmmaker first. I’ve filmed 200+ weddings since 2017, ran production at a credit union with a team of 35+, and hold an FAA Part 107 drone certificate. That last part matters: the aerial shots on this channel are flown legally, with the paperwork, in places where the paperwork is required.

What that means for you: the trip films look like films. Trucks on dunes, alpine lakes from 400 feet, campsites at blue hour. I cut everything in DaVinci Resolve. No stock footage. No “cinematic” preset packs.

FAQ

Can a Cybertruck really do a long road trip?

Yes. My biggest trips have run over 6,000 miles, and I’ve put 70,000+ miles on the Cybertruck platform. Michigan to Glacier National Park and back took 9 days, camping in the bed. The constraint is charger spacing in Montana and the Dakotas, not the truck. The article has the route and the charging stops.

Do you camp in the truck or tow a trailer?

In the truck. Bed, tonneau closed, 48V power for Starlink and lights. No trailer. Range matters more than square footage.

Is your drone footage legal in national parks?

Drones are banned inside national parks, so no. Everything you see near Glacier was flown outside park boundaries under Part 107 rules. Dunes, Crown land, and Michigan beaches are a different story, and I fly where it’s allowed.
