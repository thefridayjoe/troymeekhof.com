Lake Michigan Beach-side Wedding

A ceremony on the sand, wind off the lake, and a couple who wanted to remember what it sounded like, not just what it looked like. This is one of the films I point to when someone asks what a Mid-July Media highlights film is.

The day

Location: on Lake Michigan. There is no time limit on my coverage, so I don’t watch the clock and neither does the couple.

Beach weddings are beautiful and hostile to cameras in equal measure. Sand, wind, and a sun that sets directly behind the ceremony. All three had to be planned for.

The approach

Audio first. Wind on a beach will destroy a ceremony recording if you let it. Two lav mics (groom and officiant), a recorder on the DJ’s board, and a backup on-camera shotgun with a dead cat the size of a small animal. Vows are the reason the film exists. If I lose them, the drone shots don’t matter.

Two cameras. One on the couple, one on the reaction. A wedding is half what happens and half how people respond to it. One camera gets you the ceremony. Two gets you the mother of the bride.

Drone. I’m a Part 107 certified pilot, and Lake Michigan from 300 feet is the reason to use one. Establishing shots at the top, one pass during the ceremony from a legal and quiet distance, and a sunset shot for the ending. That’s it. Three drone shots in a film beat thirty.

Stay out of the way. I’m the only videographer at every Mid-July Media wedding. No associate shooters, no crew. One person with two cameras is easy to forget about, which is the point.

The cut

Edited in DaVinci Resolve.

The film is built around the vows and the toasts. Real words from real people in the room carry the story. Music sits under them, not over them. The wide beach shots are punctuation, not the sentence.

What the couple got

  • Highlights film, delivered online for easy sharing
  • Full ceremony, start to finish, with clean multi-track audio
  • Full toasts
  • Drone stills from the ceremony site
