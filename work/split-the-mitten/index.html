Split the Mitten

Max Polakovich ran 400 miles across Michigan in eight days, from Michiana to Mackinaw City, to raise money for AYA Youth Collective. I filmed it, produced it, and cut it into a 72-minute feature documentary. Here is how it went.

Client
Max Polakovich, for AYA Youth Collective
Role
Director, cinematographer, producer, editor
Year
2025
Deliverables
  • Feature documentary (72 minutes)
  • Social cutdowns (vertical)
  • Trailer
  • Still photography for press

The brief

Max had one goal: run the length of the Lower Peninsula, Michiana to Mackinaw City, on foot, from May 23 to May 30, 2025. The run raised money for AYA Youth Collective, a Grand Rapids nonprofit serving young people experiencing homelessness or housing instability. My goal was smaller and harder to define. Make a film that people who don’t run would watch to the end.

A 400-mile run is a bad subject for a highlight reel. Nothing “happens” for hours. The story is in the boring parts. Blisters at mile 140. The gas station breakfast at 5 a.m. The look on a support crew’s face when the day’s mileage gets pushed back again. That is what I built the shot list around.

The shoot

The run set the schedule, not the camera. I chased a man doing about 50 miles a day.

The kit was deliberately small. Two cameras, one drone (I hold a Part 107), a wireless lav on Max, and a shotgun for the crew. Audio-first, always. If I have Max’s breathing and his crew’s radio chatter, I have a film. If I only have pretty B-roll of a runner on a county road, I have a screensaver.

The drone did the work of showing scale. Michigan is flatter than it looks on a map, and a person on a two-lane road is invisible from 400 feet up. That was the point. One runner, one state.

The cut

Final runtime: 72 minutes. Edited in DaVinci Resolve.

The structure is mile markers, not days. Days blur together on a run like this; the numbers don’t. Every act break lands on a distance. It gave the audience a scoreboard, which is the one thing endurance sports usually forget to give people.

I cut the interviews last. The interviews explain the run. The footage is the run. When the two disagreed, the footage won.

Result

  • The film premiered on October 23, 2025 at the Wealthy Theatre in Grand Rapids, to about 60 people.
  • It is public on YouTube, on Max’s channel: Split the Mitten: My 400-Mile Run Across Michigan in 8 Days.
  • The Grand Rapids Marketing Co. write-up: Split the Mitten: watch the feature-length documentary.
  • Short-form from the run: a reel on @maxpolakovich and the reel MLive posted on @mlivenews.
  • Press coverage of the run itself (not the film): MLive coverage of the run, WZZM 13 on the finish, and FOX 17 before the start.

What I took from it: long-form documentary is mostly logistics. The camera is the easy part. Knowing where the runner will be at 6:40 a.m. on day six, and being there with charged batteries, is the job.
